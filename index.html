Water Conservation Awareness

Small, consistent student habits prevent thousands of liters of campus water wastage every semester. Commit to the actions below to log your pledge.

Your Session Progress: 0 of 5 actions practiced
01

Turn off taps properly.

Check that washroom and lab taps are completely closed after washing hands. Even a slow drip can waste over 15 liters a day.

02

Report leaking taps.

Do not assume someone else has noticed. Log leaking taps, running flushes, or faulty coolers immediately so maintenance can act.

03

Avoid unnecessary water use.

Turn off the tap while lathering soap or brushing teeth in hostels, and avoid letting drinking coolers run needlessly.

04

Use only the required amount of water.

Fill your bottle or cup with only what you plan to drink instead of pouring out half-full bottles at campus coolers.

05

Encourage others to save water.

Share polite reminders with peers and hostel mates. Visible peer habits are one of the strongest drivers of campus behavior change.

Project Information

BU100 SDG Action Lab documentation outlining our problem scope, methodology, limitations, and next steps.

1. Problem Statement

Routine water wastage on campus—including dripping washroom faucets, taps left running, and malfunctioning water coolers—accumulates into significant avoidable water loss. Many students notice these issues but lack a quick, visible way to report them or are unsure whom to inform, leading to delayed repairs and low everyday conservation awareness.

2. SDG 6 Connection

This project aligns with UN Sustainable Development Goal 6: Clean Water and Sanitation, specifically Target 6.4 (increasing water-use efficiency) and community participation in responsible water management. Encouraging sustainable daily habits among students creates measurable local action toward a global resource challenge.

3. Our Intervention

Save Every Drop is a lightweight, browser-based digital intervention combining three core elements: (1) a 30-second campus water wastage reporting form, (2) five actionable conservation tips with interactive pledges, and (3) a 5-question student awareness survey paired with a transparent impact dashboard and CSV export.

4. How We Collected Evidence

Evidence is collected through two primary channels: campus observation/wastage logs submitted via the reporting form (with optional photographs) and structured responses from our 5-question student survey. All sample entries displayed during initial testing are explicitly tagged as Demo Data and separated from verified field entries in both the interface and exported CSV files.

5. Impact Measurement

Project impact is evaluated using clear, observable indicators: total verified wastage reports logged, most frequent issue categories and campus hotspots, number of student water-saving pledges recorded, and changes in student awareness or reporting willingness between baseline (before) and follow-up (after) survey rounds.

6. Limitations

  • Uses browser localStorage rather than a central server, so records stay on the device used to submit them unless exported and merged as CSV.
  • No direct automated integration with campus facility maintenance ticketing systems yet.
  • Self-reported survey answers may reflect stated intent rather than long-term observed behavior.

7. Future Improvements

  • Connect the reporting form to a shared cloud database or campus maintenance email workflow for real-time repair notifications.
  • Place QR code posters next to washrooms and water coolers linking directly to the reporting form with pre-filled locations.
  • Add monthly campus water-meter tracking in collaboration with administration to quantify actual liters saved.
